# Psykinetic — Concept Sketches

Oct 25, 2026 · @Jeff Quigley

Two brainstormed concepts that answer one question: how a persistent co-op world gives players real progress without the loot/XP treadmill or statflation. **Status: ideas, not decisions.** Nothing here is committed or built yet.

The short version: progress lives in the world and in who you become, not in bigger numbers. Concept 1 makes the world change and remember what you did. Concept 2 replaces the character sheet with a fingerprint of how you play.

## Concept 1: Living History

The world changes for everyone, and everyone in it remembers what you did. Being known is the reward.

### Contested zones

- A zone outside the Wilds (the castle, for example) is held by one side. A real-time battle decides who holds it.
- If the Blood Rot win, the zone is goblin-held. Passage, the trade route and the dwarven forge beneath stay closed.
- Winning it back flips the zone to human-held **for everyone**: passage opens, trade resumes, the forge is reachable.
- If the defenses fall again, it reverts to goblin-held until enough players break them. The world swings; it never caps.
- What makes defenses fall is open: time, world events, or neglect (nobody funding the garrison). Neglect gives town investment real stakes.

### Dream replays and echoes

- Players who missed a battle can enter it as a **dream or memory** (sleep at the inn, the temple, a dreamwalker resident).
- The original players are there as **echoes**, snapshotted in the state they were in, doing what they did.
- Battles are hand-authored since they are replayed many times. Variety comes from a **battle moveset** (reinforcement waves, a ram at the gate, a shaman ritual, a wall breach, a flank from the mine) picked and timed by a director, like an Elden Ring boss choosing from its moveset, plus a few big variants (night assault, tunnel, rain).
- When an echo's recorded action no longer fits (target dead, path blocked), the AI takes over using that player's fingerprint (Concept 2), so the echo acts like them.
- Failing in the dream costs nothing. You wake up.
- Lore hook from the dream: death isn't death, it's a shift between realities. Echoes are other versions of real players.

### Turning the tide

- Win a lost battle in the dream and the zone flips in the waking world.
- **Everyone who fought** (original fighters and the replay party) gets a share of the spoils: a gold deposit waiting in their bank when they next log in.
- Someone in the world tells them who turned it.
- **Won battles stay replayable.** You can still live a defense you missed. Doing it *better* (saving the guard who died, sparing the wall that burned) could carry a small real effect without changing the outcome.
- Each new fall becomes the next snapshot, so replays always reflect the latest history.

### An adapting enemy

- The Blood Rot grow **different, not just stronger**: brutes that resist shoving after losing to shoves, shields after losing to archers, siege engines, tunnels, night attacks.
- New adaptations become new moves in the battle's moveset.
- Time works both ways: the longer goblins hold a zone, the more they fortify it; the longer humans hold it, the longer goblins gather.
- The Blood Rot can run as a faction agent: it reads the history and picks its next move from a whitelist.
- Named goblins (Grizzle Foot) survive, get promoted and come back. Killing one is a deed; dying to one is a grudge.
- A bigger win elsewhere (striking the tower or the star) could knock them back and reset their escalation.

### The deeds ledger

A record of facts per player, fed into companion and resident context. Facts, not lines.

- *Slew Grizzle Foot at the Defense of Umbrage.*
- *Held the east gate alone until reinforcements came.*
- *Carried Nix out of the barrow when she fell.*

Companions bring these up in their own words. The ledger stays exact; companions and the bard may embellish in character.

### Badges

- Badges like **Defense of Umbrage: Level I**, with doubling requirements: 1 for I, 2 for II, 4 for III, 8 for IV, and so on.
- Doubling makes a natural soft cap. High badges are rare, so they mark veterans.
- Badges grant recognition only, never power: a title, how residents treat you, a banner device.

### Fame

- **Weapons remember**: the sword that killed Grizzle Foot gets a name and history.
- **Trophies** go up in town: the draugr's axe in the temple, Grizzle Foot's banner in the tavern.
- **Places get named** after deeds (Wren's Stand); defenders' names are carved into the outpost wall.
- **Scars** from notable wounds stay on your character.
- **A bard** writes songs about real deeds, performed over the game's own music.
- **Stories drift**: residents retell events slightly wrong until the folk version becomes legend; the lawspeaker keeps the true record.
- **Children** reenact battles in town.
- **Residents name you** from what you do ("Shoves-the-Goblins"), not from a menu.
- **A town chronicle** in the archive, readable in game and possibly public on the website.
- **Retired heroes** become AI-run residents with their full ledger.
- **Anniversaries**: a festival on the date of a big battle, with the replay open to all.

### Shame

- Doing nothing is a deed too. Sit through a battle with no damage, heals, kills or assists and the record says so: **Coward of Umbrage**. No anti-AFK rule needed.
- The town notices, the bard gets comic material, the goblins mock you in war chants, and your companion saw the whole thing.
- The ledger tells kinds of nothing apart: hiding, fleeing the field, retreating while carrying someone (a deed), and unglamorous work like bandaging and holding a door (respected).
- **Redemption**: the stain stays in the chronicle, but the story grows. "The coward who came back" can beat a plain hero's story.
- Some players will embrace infamy. That's roleplay you didn't have to design.

### Smelling fear

- The Blood Rot smell fear: from your record, from how you're fighting right now (backpedaling, low stamina, panic-spinning), and from unbandaged wounds.
- They hunt the weak link, taunt you by your shame title in ALL CAPS, and young lieutenants go after the coward for an easy deed.
- They hesitate near famous heroes.
- Emergent tactic: players learn the goblins always go for the coward and use them as bait for an ambush.

## Concept 2: The Fingerprint

Stats are paired, give-and-take axes everyone has. Neither pole is good or bad. Every decision nudges an axis, and each player ends up with a unique fingerprint from how they play.

### The axes

The rule that makes it work: **both poles must be useful.**

| Axis | One pole | The other pole |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fear / Courage | Fear is alertness: hear footsteps, sense ambushes, run faster, move quietly. Natural scouts. | Courage holds ground: steady aim, hard to push, morale for those nearby. |
| Mind / Matter | Magic, perception, reading people. | Mass, shoving power, toughness. Ties to body archetypes. |
| Greed / Generosity | Better haggling, a nose for treasure, bribe and be bribed, some goblins will deal. | Loyal residents and companions, faster town growth, help offered unasked. |
| Natural / Scientific | Herbs, animals, tracking, terrain, the old places. | Mechanisms, traps, siege engines, alchemy, the dwarven forge. |
| Faith / Reason | Temple, blessings and resurrection mean more; sense spirits and the draugr; steadier in dark places. | See through illusions and dark-elven tricks, understand old designs, resist curses. |
| Deceit / Truth | Bluff sentries, disguise, cheat at games, get what honesty can't; spot other liars. | Your word is believed, the lawspeaker trusts you, oaths carry weight, companions follow readily. |

More candidates: Mercy / Ruthlessness, Solitary / Social, Honor / Cunning, Patience / Haste.

### Fear / Courage in detail

The moment-to-moment axis, maintained by feats in battle.

- **Raises it:** kills, shoving a goblin off a wall, a perfect parry, holding a gate, bandaging someone under fire, carrying an ally out, a war cry, standing near a hero, turning on whatever hunted you.
- **Drains it:** retreating, heavy hits, watching an ally fall, being surrounded, darkness, unbandaged wounds, a named enemy arriving, goblins chanting your shame title, low stamina.
- **At the bottom:** you break and rout briefly. **At the top:** a *stand*, a short heroic state where you can't be pushed and nearby goblins break.
- **Shown without a bar:** breathing, heartbeat, posture, companion comments, and the music swelling or tightening.
- Fear clouds the mind, so casters' spells falter.
- Resets toward a baseline after battle; your deeds set the baseline.

### Different time scales

- **Within a fight:** Fear / Courage.
- **Over a session:** Mind / Matter, as your body changes.
- **Over a career:** Greed / Generosity, Faith / Reason, Deceit / Truth.

### How decisions nudge axes

- **What you do:** spare or kill a goblin, share or keep loot, pray before a fight or study the enemy.
- **What you say:** free-text speech to residents and companions, classified by the AI (honest, boastful, merciful, scheming). Lies can be caught: claim you killed Grizzle Foot when the ledger says you hid, and those who were there know.
- **How you live:** what you eat, where you rest, whether you tend your companion's wounds, who you spend time with.

### What the fingerprint does

- **Archetypes emerge** from combinations, never picked from a menu: fear + greed + cunning is a thief; courage + generosity + faith is a paladin; mind + science + patience is an artificer.
- **A sigil:** a crest generated from your fingerprint, on your shield and banner.
- **Your echo:** drives how your echo behaves when the AI takes over in a replay.
- **Your legacy:** shapes how a retired hero thinks as a resident.
- **The world reacts:** dwarves respect science, druids nature, the draugr courage; the Blood Rot respect greed and ruthlessness and may deal. Doors open by character.
- **The enemy studies it:** the Blood Rot bribe the greedy, bait the hasty, frighten the fearful.
- **No sheet of numbers:** you learn who you are from your companion, what residents call you, how merchants treat you, and the music.

### Companions with convictions

- **A starting shape** from the companion card (birthplace, religion, parentage, profession): a temple orphan leans faithful, a smuggler leans deceit and greed. Body and build follow.
- **Core convictions vs. drift:** a few axes they hold no matter what (she keeps the faith); others drift with experience.
- **Influence runs both ways:** travel with a truthful companion and your truth axis creeps up.
- **Fit creates synergy:** a faithful companion deepens blessings; a deceitful one backs your bluff; a truthful one may contradict your lie in front of the barkeep; a fearful scout complements a brave player.
- **Clashes create story:** crises of faith, quiet disapproval, and defection with a reason, to the temple, the dwarves, or the Blood Rot.
- **Some only join the right person:** a devout companion won't follow a known liar; others join *to* change you.
- **Their arcs are deeds:** "Nix kept the faith at Umbrage when everyone else fled" goes in the ledger, and residents remember her for it.

## How they connect, and what to test first

Both concepts run on one shared record of what actually happened. Living History writes deeds into the ledger; the Fingerprint reads how you did them. Together they form the loop:

1. Something happens in the shared world (a raid, a lost castle, the barrow).
2. You take part, live or in a dream replay.
3. The world changes for everyone (a zone flips, the town grows, an axe hangs on the temple wall).
4. Your ledger and fingerprint record what you did and how.
5. Everyone knows: companions, residents, the bard, the Blood Rot.
6. The enemy adapts, and the next event is different.

It avoids statflation because every axis is bounded with a cost at each pole, badges grant recognition rather than power, and zones swing instead of capping.

### Open questions

- What makes a held zone fall again, and how often?
- Does joining a *failed* replay earn anything?
- How is the dream replay entered, and what does it look like in game?
- Which axes ship first, and how many total?
- How are axis positions shown to the player without numbers?
- Does escalation ever reset, and what triggers it?

- [ ] Fear / Courage in one defense battle or the barrow: does the music and posture make courage felt?
- [ ] The barrow as a full turn of the loop: area opens, town changes (axe on the wall), companion moment, a ledger entry she brings up later.
