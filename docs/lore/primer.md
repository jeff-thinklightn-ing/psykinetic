## Tone

*Gísla saga* is the reference: feuds that outlive the people who started them,
outlaws, named weapons, grudges, omens and dreams, and loyalty that holds when it
costs everything. Dark like *Diablo* 1, but restrained in how it is shown:
terrible things are stated plainly and found as aftermath, never depicted. People
are plain-spoken. Fate is felt, not explained. Nobody in the world thinks of
themselves as living in a story, and the world has no modern words for what it
does not understand.

## The land

The land's name is not settled *(asked: A1)*. It is old stone places (rooms and
corridors lit by lanterns and fires) and the open ground around them: fields,
farmsteads and forest. Its people give directions as north, south, east and west.

Monsters prowl the stone places and the open ground, and they attack whoever they
see. Fire burns whoever stands in it.

Raiders can kill, but whoever falls, townsfolk too, can be brought back (Jeff).
The town has a temple, kept by a priest, and out in the wilds stands a healer's
hut where a medicine man lives; the fallen are brought back at either. The temple
and the town follow Theus, the one god of their homeland (Jeff, 2026-10-22). Whether being brought back costs anything or marks
the one brought back, and whether people speak of it are open *(asked: B1)*.

No one has been knighted in ages. Whether there were ever knights in the land,
and who made them, is open *(asked: A4, H1)*.

How the land is governed and what its laws are *(asked: A3, A4)*, how real magic
is and what "the gift" is *(asked: B2)* are open. Beside Theus there are the old gods, a tribe's many gods, whose god
of war Psyke empowers the barrow's draugr; few know it.

## The Blood Rot's land

The region around the town is the Blood Rot's land. At its heart is an old mine.
The land around the mine is dead: no crops, no game, nothing living. Farther out,
toward the town, there are fields and forest, and in the forests the Blood Rot's
raiders keep small camps. The whole region lives under constant threat from those
raiders. When their numbers grow too high, the raids spill into the regions
beyond.

## The town

The town is where the players and their companions belong. It is named Umbrage
(Jeff, 2026-10-24: "i want to change the name of the village to Umbrage, it actually fits,
the town is cursed because of the grave robbers and the economy that runs off the barrows
gold"; it was named Held, 2026-10-18). Umbrage is cursed: everyone says so, and some say
why, the grave robbers and an economy that runs off the barrow's gold. The name belongs
to the town and goes with it (Jeff, 2026-10-24: "the town that is near the barrows and has
the curse is called Umbrage, even if it moves"). It lies in the Blood Rot's land, at a distance from the mine.

In draft until Jeff checks the wording (2026-10-22): Umbrage sits in the Woodlands. Across the
river stands the Old Tower, a broken-down castle the ancients lived in. It was conquered in
the battle, and its fallen warriors were buried (in the Barrow of the Fallen) before the
people moved on; nothing is known of them since. Snagfin the goblin keeps his counter
there. An old farmstead in the wilds stands abandoned, with tools and bandages inside.

Everything found on the road (gold, ore, wood, cloth, food, weapons and gear)
comes home to the town's shared stockpile, and the town grows from what is
brought back, not one person's wealth. People rescued from captivity come to live
in the town and work their trade there. Ordinary iron is found near the town.

Who rules or speaks for the town is open *(asked: A4)*.

## The Blood Rot

The Blood Rot are a band of goblins who live in and around the old mine. Their
children are born changed, and that is why they are called the Blood Rot. The
nearer to the mine one of them lives, the stronger and more changed it is. What
the rest of the land calls a curse, they have grown to thrive in. They took the
name as their own, and to them it is a creed, not an insult.

The rot kept their enemies away, and they worship its source: a fallen star in
the deep of the mine. Because the land around the mine is dead, they cannot feed
themselves from it. They raid outside it for food and game, and their high
priests demand bounty and sacrifice for the star. Their raiders take food, game
and people. Those taken are carried down toward the star and are not seen again.

## The fallen star and the tower

Deep in the mine lies the source of the rot: a star that fell into the land many
thousands of years ago, before the town and before the clan. It has different
names depending on who speaks. Common folk call it a fallen star. Those who live
near the mine call it the rot-source. The learned have tied the rot to the ore of
the mine, some by watching and some by the gift. Blighted ore comes from there.
What it does when worked is open *(asked: D3)*.

A tower stands over the mine. In Jeff's second session it was built by orcish labour
to the designs of dark elven architects, in their majestic style, and the earlier
humans were dropped: the world is full of mythical peoples, and the tower is theirs
*(draft until Jeff confirms the wording)*. The goblins grew stronger from the rot.
The tower holds old artifacts and treasures, and dark elven artifacts come from it.
Its inside shifts, perhaps by magic; its treasures have never all been carried out,
and adventurers still come. Whether the dark elves died of the rot, left or still
live, and whether they or the orcs worshipped the star before the goblins, are
open.

## The smith

In the roadmap's first scenario the smith is taken, and his hammer with him, and
the players bring him home. Jeff (2026-10-22): the smith heard rumours of a great
dwarven forge deep below the castle and went down after it, bringing an expedition of
hired guards to protect him and carry out what they found. They were ambushed and he
was captured. He is held at the forge, forced to work goblin weapons that supply the
Blood Rot's raids. The smith's name, and whether his hammer has one, are open
*(asked: E3)*.

## The castle and the labyrinths

From Jeff's second session (`castle.md`), draft until he confirms the wording. The
castle is ancient and dilapidated, built by dwarven stone smiths, plain and
utilitarian, over a known entrance to the labyrinths, to guard it. It guards a
treasure that was rumoured and never found. It is no longer dwarven, and it is
mostly overrun; by whom is open *(asked: A2)*. The maps of the labyrinths were lost
long ago, and the labyrinths shift, which is why their treasures were never carried
out and why adventurers still come. Dwarven artifacts come from the dungeons below.

## The goblins in play and their allies

Three kinds are in the game today, and everyone in the land knows them (Jeff,
2026-10-17). Imps are goblins, and every goblin in the Blood Rot's land is of the
Blood Rot: the region is dominated by Blood Rot goblins. Brutes are orcs and
sneaks are kobolds, peoples allied to the Blood Rot. The imp is small and darts in
and out. The brute is big and heavy, and it stands guard. The sneak patrols and
comes at its prey from the side.

The allies do not worship the fallen star. They are with the Blood Rot for what it
gets them, so the alliance can break, and one of them can be turned. Kobolds dig
the mine's shafts for the Blood Rot; orcs fight for a share of what the raids bring
in. Whether the allies who live near the mine are changed by the rot is open
*(proposal: yes, and those at the edges are not)*. Goblins win by holding things
(captives and ground), not by taking loot from the people they fight *(draft)*.
Whether goblins can be bargained with is open *(asked: C1)*. Orcs and kobolds need
faction keys in `levels/factions.json` from maps before entries of their own.

Whether there are other factions (outlaws, a rival steading) is open *(asked: E1)*.

## Beasts of the wilds

Deer and rabbits graze in the wilds and flee together from what they see or
hear. A stag is a deer with antlers. A boar flees, but charges when it is
cornered. Wolves hunt deer and rabbits, and they are more dangerous at night (Jeff). A
hunter takes meat and hides from them, antlers from a stag and tusks from a boar;
from a wolf, only its hide.

## The companions

Companions travel with the players by choice. Who each one is (temper, manner,
where she comes from) is in her card, which the companions tree makes: by Jeff's
ruling on E2, cards are rolled for every companion, and a card grows from what
she witnesses and what is said to her. Lore doesn't write companions' pasts. It
gives the world they are rolled into.

Wren, Nix and Pip stay as named companions (Jeff). Wren and Nix are women. Pip is
a young man who wants to become a squire, and can't: no one has been knighted in
ages. All three rest in town between
trips. Beyond that, who each one is comes from their own card.

## Who knows what

Lore is local. Townsfolk know their own surroundings and the rumours told there.
The learned know more of the star and the ore. A scholar knows
history (whether there is an archive and a scholar yet is open, *asked: E4*).
Each companion knows what she has lived through. Every fact in `levels/lore/`
says who knows it, so a companion only says what her character would know.
