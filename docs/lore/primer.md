# World primer

Status: **draft**. This page states only what has been decided: the roadmap's
first scenario, the companions' world text (`VOICE_WORLD` in `sim/companion.gd`)
and the tone. Everything else is left open and marked *(asked: X)*, where X is the
question's code in the list Jeff and Dire are answering (`docs/lore_questions.md`
in the main checkout). The structured facts behind this page are in
`levels/lore/*.json` (format: `docs/lore/README.md`); where the two disagree, the
data is wrong and gets fixed.

## Tone

*Gísla saga* is the reference: feuds that outlive the people who started them,
outlaws, named weapons, grudges, omens and dreams, and loyalty that holds when it
costs everything. People are plain-spoken. Fate is felt, not explained. Nobody in
the world thinks of themselves as living in a story.

## The land

The land's name is not settled *(asked: A1)*. It is old stone places (rooms and
corridors lit by lanterns and fires) and the open ground around them: fields,
farmsteads and forest. Its people give directions as north, south, east and west.

Monsters prowl the stone places and the open ground, and they attack whoever they
see. Fire burns whoever stands in it.

Whoever falls rises again after a while. It hurts, and no one wants to fall. Why
this happens, and whether anyone in the world gives it a name, is open *(asked: B1)*.

How the land is governed and what its laws are *(asked: A3, A4)*, how real magic is
*(asked: B2)* and which gods are worshipped *(asked: B3)* are all open.

## The town

The town is where the players and their companions belong. Its name is open
*(asked: A1)*. Everything found on the road (gold, ore, wood, cloth, food,
weapons and gear) comes home to the town's shared stockpile, and the town grows
from what is brought back, not one person's wealth. People rescued from captivity
come to live in the town and work their trade there.

Ordinary iron is found near the town.

## The castle and the raid

A castle stands near the town, and the goblins have overrun it. Whose castle it
was, and when it fell, are open *(asked: A2)*. It has a keep.

The goblins raided a farmstead near the castle and took the smith, and his hammer
with him. They hold him in the castle's keep. Between the farmstead and the castle
lie fields. Why they took him, and why the hammer, is open *(asked: C3)*. The smith's
name, and whether his hammer has one, are open *(asked: E3)*.

## The threats

**The goblins** hold the castle and raid the land around it. They win by holding
things (captives and ground), not by taking loot from the people they fight. The
town knows three kinds of them. The imp is small and darts in and out. The brute is
big and heavy, and it guards. The sneak patrols and comes at you from the side.
Whether the goblins are a people who can be bargained with or beasts *(asked: C1)*,
and whether the three kinds are one people or creatures kept by the goblins
*(asked: C2)*, are open.

**The Blood Rot** are a clan whose lands lie under a blight. Blighted ore comes
from there. Who or what the Blood Rot are *(asked: D1)*, what the blight is *(asked:
D2)* and what blighted ore does when worked *(asked: D3)* are open.

Whether there are other factions (outlaws, a rival steading) is open *(asked: E1)*.

## The companions

Wren, Pip and Nix travel with the players by choice. Who each one is (temper and
manner) is in their cards (`levels/companions.json`, owned by the companions
tree). Where they come from, and how they came to the town, are open *(asked: E2)*.

## Who knows what

Lore is local. Townsfolk know their own surroundings and the rumours told there. A
scholar knows history (whether there is an archive and a scholar yet is open,
*asked: E4*). Each companion knows what she has lived through. Every fact in
`levels/lore/` says who knows it, so a companion only says what her character would
know.
