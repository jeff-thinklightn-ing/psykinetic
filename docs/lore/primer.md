# World primer

Status: **draft**. This page states only what has been decided: the roadmap's
first scenario, the companions' world text (`VOICE_WORLD` in `sim/companion.gd`),
the tone, and Jeff's points in `docs/lore/blood_rot.md` (his Blood Rot starter,
where each point is marked [Jeff] or [proposal]; only [Jeff] points are stated
here). Everything else is left open and marked *(asked: X)*, where X is the
question's code in the list Jeff and Dire are answering (`docs/lore_questions.md`
in the main checkout). The structured facts behind this page are in
`levels/lore/*.json` (format: `docs/lore/README.md`); where the two disagree, the
data is wrong and gets fixed. Nothing is confirmed until Jeff and Dire say so.

## Tone

*Gísla saga* is the reference: feuds that outlive the people who started them,
outlaws, named weapons, grudges, omens and dreams, and loyalty that holds when it
costs everything. Dark like *Diablo* 1, but restrained in how it is shown:
terrible things are stated plainly and found as aftermath, never depicted. People
are plain-spoken. Fate is felt, not explained. Nobody in the world thinks of
themselves as living in a story, and the world has no modern words for what it
does not understand.

## The land

The land's name is not settled *(asked: A1)*. It is old stone places (rooms and
corridors lit by lanterns and fires) and the open ground around them: fields,
farmsteads and forest. Its people give directions as north, south, east and west.

Monsters prowl the stone places and the open ground, and they attack whoever they
see. Fire burns whoever stands in it.

Whoever falls rises again after a while. It hurts, and no one wants to fall. Why
this happens, and whether any death is one the fallen do not rise from, is open
*(asked: B1)*.

How the land is governed and what its laws are *(asked: A3, A4)*, how real magic
is and what "the gift" is *(asked: B2)*, and which gods are worshipped *(asked:
B3)* are all open.

## The Blood Rot's land

The region around the town is the Blood Rot's land. At its heart is an old mine.
The land around the mine is dead: no crops, no game, nothing living. Farther out,
toward the town, there are fields and forest, and in the forests the Blood Rot's
raiders keep small camps. The whole region lives under constant threat from those
raiders. When their numbers grow too high, the raids spill into the regions
beyond.

## The town

The town is where the players and their companions belong. Its name is open
*(asked: A1)*. It lies in the Blood Rot's land, at a distance from the mine.

Everything found on the road (gold, ore, wood, cloth, food, weapons and gear)
comes home to the town's shared stockpile, and the town grows from what is
brought back, not one person's wealth. People rescued from captivity come to live
in the town and work their trade there. Ordinary iron is found near the town.

Who rules or speaks for the town is open *(asked: A4)*.

## The Blood Rot

The Blood Rot are a band of goblins who live in and around the old mine. Their
children are born changed, and that is why they are called the Blood Rot. The
nearer to the mine one of them lives, the stronger and more changed it is. What
the rest of the land calls a curse, they have grown to thrive in. They took the
name as their own, and to them it is a creed, not an insult.

The rot kept their enemies away, and they worship its source: a fallen star in
the deep of the mine. Because the land around the mine is dead, they cannot feed
themselves from it. They raid outside it for food and game, and their high
priests demand bounty and sacrifice for the star. Their raiders take food, game
and people. Those taken are carried down toward the star and are not seen again.

## The fallen star and the tower

Deep in the mine lies the source of the rot: a star that fell into the land many
thousands of years ago, before the town and before the clan. It has different
names depending on who speaks. Common folk call it a fallen star. Those who live
near the mine call it the rot-source. The learned have tied the rot to the ore of
the mine, some by watching and some by the gift. Blighted ore comes from there.
What it does when worked is open *(asked: D3)*.

A tower stands over the mine. It was built by humans who lived before. They died
off from the rot, and the goblins grew stronger from it. The tower still holds
their artifacts and treasures. Who those humans were *(asked: H1)*, and whether
they worshipped the star too *(asked: H2)*, are open.

## The smith

In the roadmap's first scenario the smith is taken, and his hammer with him, and
the players bring him home. The first draft had goblins take him from a
farmstead and hold him in an overrun castle's keep. That castle was a
placeholder. Who takes him now, from where and why, is open *(asked: C3)*, as is
whether there is a castle at all *(asked: A2)*. The smith's name, and whether his
hammer has one, are open *(asked: E3)*.

## The goblins in play

Three goblin kinds are in the game today. The imp is small and darts in and out.
The brute is big and heavy, and it stands guard. The sneak patrols and comes at
its prey from the side. Goblins win by holding things (captives and ground), not by
taking loot from the people they fight. How these goblins stand to the Blood Rot
(the same clan, kin in a feud, or unrelated) is open *(asked: H4)*, as is whether
the three kinds are ranks of one people *(asked: C2)* and whether goblins can be
bargained with *(asked: C1)*.

Whether there are other factions (outlaws, a rival steading) is open *(asked: E1)*.

## The companions

Companions travel with the players by choice. Who each one is (temper, manner,
where she comes from) is in her card, which the companions tree makes: by Jeff's
ruling on E2, cards are rolled for every companion, and a card grows from what
she witnesses and what is said to her. Lore doesn't write companions' pasts. It
gives the world they are rolled into.

Wren, Nix and Pip stay as named companions (Jeff). Wren and Nix are women. Pip is
a young man who wants to become a squire. Whom he would squire for is open, since
the draft has no lord or knight *(asked: H10)*. All three rest in town between
trips. Beyond that, who each one is comes from their own card.

## Who knows what

Lore is local. Townsfolk know their own surroundings and the rumours told there.
The learned know more of the star and the ore. A scholar knows
history (whether there is an archive and a scholar yet is open, *asked: E4*).
Each companion knows what she has lived through. Every fact in `levels/lore/`
says who knows it, so a companion only says what her character would know.
