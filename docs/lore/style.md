# Names and style

Status: **draft**. How names work is open *(asked: F1)*; this page will hold the
rules once it is answered. Until then:

## Writing lore

- State facts, never lines. "The goblins took the smith" is lore; a sentence for a
  companion to say is not.
- Plain words, short sentences, concrete things: iron, smoke, a broken door. No
  modern words (no "okay", "team", "stress"), no game words (levels, spawns, XP,
  players) inside the world's facts. Nor "companion" or "owner": those who travel
  with the town's people go out with them by choice, and a fact says so.
- Tone after *Gísla saga*: understated, fated, loyal. Say what happened and leave
  the feeling to the reader.
- Dark like *Diablo* 1, restrained in the showing (Jeff): terrible things are
  stated plainly and found as aftermath (what is left lying around), never
  depicted as scenes. Changed and wrong, not gory.
- The world has no modern words for what it does not understand. Facts say what
  people see and know (a fallen star, the rot), not the cause behind it.
- One fact per statement, so each can carry its own knowledge level.
- Don't invent big things (names of the land and the town, the core history, the
  nature of the Blood Rot). Ask through main.

## Names (until F1 is answered)

- The companions already in the game go by short English bynames: Wren, Pip, Nix.
  New companions and townsfolk can follow that.
- Factions are called what people call them, with "the": the goblins, the Blood
  Rot, the town.
- Monster kinds stay as they are in the game: imp, brute, sneak.
- A named weapon or heirloom takes the name of its maker, its holder or a deed
  (as items will later name things after long-time holders: "Wren's dagger").
  The forms names take are in `levels/lore/names.json` (README, decision 366).
  Whether a named thing kept from the one it is named for, rather than given
  by them, is cursed is open
  *(asked: H12)*.

## Who knows what, and who may lie

Jeff (2026-10-17), word for word: "offical characters only speak lore facts,
priests, guards, officials -- bards, scoundrels, and thieves aren't bound to lore
facts -- they can embellish and lie but only in character, and towns people don't
fabricate they can store what they hear and decide, they may have a intillect stat
that influences how vulnerable they are to falsehoods".

So lore holds what is so (and, as rumours, what is said). Which roles are bound to
it is in `levels/lore/roles.json` (README, decision 376). A falsehood a resident
comes to believe is that resident's, never a lore fact.
