# The Blood Rot

Status: **draft**. Brainstormed by Jeff in a lore session. Jeff decides; Dire
playtests and brings ideas to Jeff. Under lore's rule a fact is `agreed` only when
Jeff said it word for word or it is already in the game, so **[Jeff]** lines here
that paraphrase him stay draft until lore checks the wording with him. This page answers, in draft, the primer's open questions
D1 (who the Blood Rot are), D2 (what the blight is) and D3 (what blighted ore does),
and touches B2 (magic) and B3 (gods).

**[Jeff]** The castle in the primer was a placeholder. The Blood Rot are the main
faction of this region. The primer's "castle and the raid" and "the goblins"

Each point is marked by where it came from:

- **[Jeff]**: Jeff said it. Draft until Jeff and Dire confirm.
- **[proposal]**: suggested in the session, not yet taken up. Open to change or cut.

Tone reminder: dark like Diablo 1, restrained in how it is shown. Terrible things
are stated plainly and found as aftermath, not depicted.

## Lore team

### Who they are

- **[Jeff]** The Blood Rot are a band of goblins who live in and around an old mine.
- **[Jeff]** Their children are born changed. This is why they are called the Blood Rot.
- **[Jeff]** The nearer to the mine a Blood Rot lives, the stronger and more changed it is.
- **[Jeff]** What the rest of the land calls a curse, the Blood Rot have grown to thrive in.
- **[Jeff]** They took the name "Blood Rot" as their own. To them it is a creed, not an insult.
- **[Jeff]** The rot kept their enemies away. They worship its source.

### The fallen star

- **[Jeff]** Deep in the mine lies the source: a star that fell into the land many
  thousands of years ago. It is older than the castle, the town and the clan.
- **[Jeff]** It has different names depending on who speaks. Common folk: a fallen
  star. Those near the mine: the rot-source. The learned have tied the curse to the
  ore, some by watching and some by the gift.
- The world has no modern words for what it is. Facts describe what people see and
  know, not the cause behind it.

### Faith and hunger

- **[Jeff]** Nothing can live near the mine. The land around it is dead, so the Blood
  Rot cannot feed themselves from it.
- **[Jeff]** They raid outside their land for food and game.
- **[Jeff]** Their high priests demand bounty and sacrifice for the fallen star.
- **[Jeff]** The raiders take food, game and people. Those taken are carried down
  toward the star and are not seen again.

### The region and the town

- **[Jeff]** The starter town lies in the Blood Rot's region, at a distance from the mine.
- **[Jeff]** The whole region lives under constant threat from Blood Rot raiders.
- **[Jeff]** The raiders keep small camps in the forests.
- **[Jeff]** When their numbers grow too high, the raids spill into neighbouring regions.

### Allies

- **[Jeff]** The first region is dominated by Blood Rot goblins.
- **[Jeff]** The Blood Rot may have other factions allied to them. For example, the
  brutes might be orcs and the sneaks might be kobolds.
- **[proposal]** The allies do not worship the star. They are with the Blood Rot for
  what it gets them, so an alliance can break, and one can be turned.
- **[proposal]** Kobolds dig. They could be the ones who work the shafts for the
  Blood Rot, or who dug toward the star in the first place.
- **[proposal]** Orcs are paid muscle, paid in a share of what the raids bring in.
- **[proposal]** Open: are the allies touched by the rot? If they live near the mine
  they would be changed too; if they keep to the edges, they stay as they are.

### The tower

- **[Jeff]** A tower stands over the mine. It was built by earlier humans.
- **[Jeff]** Those humans died off from the rot. The goblins grew stronger from it.
- **[Jeff]** The tower still holds the earlier humans' artifacts and treasures.
- **[proposal]** The earlier humans built over the star to claim it, as a sign of
  power. The Blood Rot now hold what they built: a holding taken by the ground itself.
- **[proposal]** Human treasures left long near the star carry the rot, like the ore.
  The best loot in the tower is also the most tainted.
- **[proposal]** Some of what the earlier humans knew survives in the tower:
  writings or carvings showing they learned too late what the star did to them.
  This is where the learned could have first tied the curse to the ore.

## Map team

- **[Jeff]** The starter town is in this region, well away from the mine.
- **[Jeff]** A tower built by earlier humans stands over the mine.
- **[proposal]** The tower is visible across the region, including from town: the
  landmark that says where the threat lives.
- **[proposal]** Going up the tower and down the mine are two halves of one place:
  human ruin above, Blood Rot depths below, the star at the bottom.
- **[Jeff]** Raider camps are scattered through the region's forests.
- **[Jeff]** Too many raiders and the raids spill into neighbouring regions.
- **[proposal]** Camps grow on the always-on world's slow tick when left alone, so
  pressure builds while no one is playing and clearing camps keeps the region in check.
  Spillover gives a reason to go back to the starter region later in the game.
- **[proposal]** Between the town and the mine, the land dies by degrees: living
  fields and forest near town, then thinning growth, then dead ground at the mine.
  The road toward the mine tells you where you are going.
- **[Jeff]** The mine is the heart of their land. Strength rises the deeper you go.
- **[Jeff]** The land around the mine is dead: no crops, no game, nothing living.
- **[proposal]** Layout reads as a descent: dead ground at the surface, upper shafts,
  middle depths, the deep, then the star itself as the final place.
- **[proposal]** Offerings change with depth, told through what is left lying around
  (aftermath, not scenes):
  - upper shafts: stores of grain and game
  - middle depths: trophies and bones
  - the deep: the altar where those carried down were brought
- **[proposal]** Raids reach out into farmsteads and fields. A captive being carried
  inward is a rescue with a clock on it, separate from the castle and the smith.
- Place entries for `regions.json` once names exist: the Blood Rot's land (region),
  the mine (place), the star's chamber (place, no knowers: no one living has seen it).

## Art team

### Rank is nearness to the star

**[proposal]** A Blood Rot rises in standing by moving deeper and surviving it.
Being more changed is a mark of rank, not shame.

| Rank | Where | Look | In a fight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raiders | edges and beyond | nearly ordinary goblins; blotched skin, sacks and ropes | fast and light; grab and run; easy to shove |
| Haulers | upper shafts | overgrown, thick-backed; carry tribute inward | slow and heavy; block tunnels with their bodies |
| Wardens | middle depths | bony growths, too many joints; guard the descent | hit hard, hard to push, hold ground |
| the Marked | near the star | faint glow, barely goblin; never leave the deep | attendants to the priests; dangerous up close |
| High priests | at the star | the most changed of all | voice of the star; boss tier |

All ranks and names are **[proposal]**; high priests are **[Jeff]**.

### Build notes

- **[proposal]** One goblin base mesh and rig, with growths, bulk and glow added per
  rank. Five readable models from one skeleton; the change is visible at a glance.
- **[proposal]** Mass rises with rank, so the shove-and-collide physics tells the
  story: raiders fly, wardens barely move. (Numbers belong to combat/economy, not lore.)
- **[Jeff]** Brutes may become orcs and sneaks kobolds: allied peoples with their
  own base meshes, not Blood Rot ranks.
- **[proposal]** The imp could stay as the Blood Rot raider, sharing the goblin base.
- **[proposal]** Allies show which side they are on through kit (Blood Rot markings,
  tribute trinkets) rather than the rot's changes, unless they live near the mine.
- Keep it restrained: changed and wrong, not gory.

### The tower

- **[Jeff]** Built by earlier humans; holds their artifacts and treasures.
- **[proposal]** Human stonework underneath, goblin additions crudely built over
  it: lashed timber, hides, offerings. Two hands, two eras, readable at a glance.
- **[proposal]** Human artifacts are a separate item style from goblin gear: finer
  make, old, and (near the star) tainted.
